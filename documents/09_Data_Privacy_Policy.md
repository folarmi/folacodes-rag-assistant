# Data Privacy Policy

## 1. Purpose

Folacodes Technologies is committed to protecting the privacy and personal information of employees, customers, vendors, job applicants, contractors, and other individuals whose personal data the company processes.

This policy establishes the principles and requirements for collecting, using, storing, sharing, retaining, and disposing of personal data in compliance with applicable data protection laws.

Related Policies:

- POL-001 Employee Handbook
- POL-002 Code of Conduct
- POL-005 Information Security Policy
- POL-006 IT Acceptable Use Policy

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- Employees
- Contractors
- Consultants
- Temporary Staff
- Interns
- Third-party service providers processing personal data on behalf of the company

It covers both electronic and paper records containing personal information.

---

# 3. Privacy Principles

Folacodes Technologies processes personal data in accordance with the following principles:

- Lawfulness
- Fairness
- Transparency
- Purpose Limitation
- Data Minimization
- Accuracy
- Storage Limitation
- Integrity
- Confidentiality
- Accountability

Employees handling personal information are expected to understand and apply these principles in their daily work.

---

# 4. What is Personal Data?

Personal data is any information that can directly or indirectly identify an individual.

Examples include:

- Full name
- Home address
- Email address
- Telephone number
- Date of birth
- National Identification Number
- Passport Number
- Driver's Licence Number
- Employee ID
- Salary Information
- Bank Account Details
- Medical Information
- IP Address
- Device Identifiers
- Photographs
- Biometric Information

---

# 5. Special Category Data

Certain personal information requires additional protection.

Examples include:

- Medical records
- Biometric information
- Disability information
- Criminal background checks
- Government-issued identity documents

Access to this information is limited to authorized personnel with a legitimate business need.

---

# 6. Lawful Basis for Processing

The company processes personal data only where there is an appropriate legal basis, including:

- Performance of a contract
- Compliance with legal obligations
- Legitimate business interests
- Protection of vital interests
- Individual consent (where required)

Personal data must not be collected "just in case" it may be useful later.

---

# 7. Collection of Personal Data

Only the minimum amount of personal information necessary for a defined business purpose should be collected.

Examples include:

- Recruitment
- Payroll
- Employee benefits
- Customer onboarding
- Contract management
- Regulatory compliance

Employees should avoid requesting unnecessary personal information.

---

# 8. Use of Personal Data

Personal data may only be used for the purpose for which it was originally collected unless another lawful basis exists.

Examples include:

- Managing employment
- Delivering products and services
- Customer support
- Regulatory reporting
- Financial processing
- Security investigations

Personal information must never be used for personal gain.

---

# 9. Sharing Personal Data

Personal information may only be shared:

- With authorized employees
- With approved service providers
- Where legally required
- Where contractually necessary

Before sharing personal data externally, employees should confirm that:

- The recipient is authorized.
- Appropriate safeguards exist.
- Only necessary information is shared.

---

# 10. International Transfers

Where personal data is transferred outside Nigeria, appropriate safeguards must be implemented to ensure an adequate level of protection.

Such transfers should be reviewed by Legal & Compliance before implementation.

---

# 11. Data Retention

Personal information should be retained only for as long as necessary to fulfill business, legal, regulatory, or contractual requirements.

The following retention periods serve as general guidance:

| Record Type                                   | Retention Period              |
| --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Recruitment records (unsuccessful applicants) | 12 months                     |
| Employee personnel files                      | 7 years after employment ends |
| Payroll records                               | 7 years                       |
| Customer contracts                            | 7 years after contract ends   |
| Procurement records                           | 7 years                       |
| Security incident records                     | 7 years                       |

Records should be securely destroyed when no longer required.

---

# 12. Secure Disposal

When personal data reaches the end of its retention period, it should be securely destroyed.

Examples include:

- Secure shredding of paper records
- Certified deletion of electronic files
- Secure destruction of storage media

Employees must not dispose of confidential records using ordinary waste bins.

---

# 13. Individual Rights

Individuals may have the right to:

- Access their personal information
- Request correction of inaccurate data
- Request deletion where legally permitted
- Restrict processing
- Object to certain processing activities
- Request a copy of their information in an appropriate format

Requests should be directed to the Legal & Compliance Department.

---

# 14. Privacy by Design

Privacy considerations should be incorporated into new systems, applications, and business processes from the earliest stages of planning.

Project teams should consider:

- Data minimization
- Access controls
- Encryption
- Retention periods
- Audit logging
- User privacy

Projects involving significant personal data processing may require a Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA).

---

# 15. Data Breach Reporting

Any suspected loss, unauthorized disclosure, or unauthorized access to personal data must be reported immediately.

Examples include:

- Sending personal data to the wrong recipient
- Lost laptop containing personal information
- Unauthorized database access
- Misconfigured cloud storage
- Stolen documents

Employees should report suspected privacy incidents within **one hour** of discovery.

Reports should be made to:

- Information Security Team
- Legal & Compliance Department
- IT Service Desk (where technology is involved)

---

# 16. Working Remotely

Employees handling personal information while working remotely should:

- Use the company VPN.
- Lock devices when unattended.
- Avoid discussing confidential information in public places.
- Prevent family members or visitors from accessing company information.
- Store paper documents securely.

---

# 17. Third-Party Processors

Before sharing personal data with a third party, the company should evaluate:

- Security controls
- Privacy practices
- Regulatory compliance
- Contractual protections
- Confidentiality obligations

Third-party access should be limited to the minimum necessary.

---

# 18. Responsibilities

## Employees

Employees are responsible for:

- Protecting personal information.
- Reporting privacy incidents promptly.
- Following this policy.
- Completing required privacy training.
- Accessing only information required for their job.

---

## Managers

Managers are responsible for:

- Reinforcing privacy practices.
- Reviewing access requirements.
- Supporting compliance activities.
- Escalating privacy concerns.

---

## Legal & Compliance

The Legal & Compliance Department is responsible for:

- Maintaining this policy.
- Advising on privacy obligations.
- Managing privacy requests.
- Supporting investigations.
- Coordinating regulatory notifications where required.

---

# 19. Privacy Do's and Don'ts

| Do                                                     | Don't                                                |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Access only information you need                       | Browse employee or customer records out of curiosity |
| Verify recipients before sending emails                | Send personal data to the wrong recipient            |
| Lock your computer when away                           | Leave confidential information visible               |
| Report suspected privacy incidents immediately         | Attempt to hide a mistake involving personal data    |
| Store personal information in approved company systems | Save company personal data to personal cloud storage |

---

# 20. Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I email customer information to my personal email account?

No. Personal information must only be stored and shared using approved company systems.

---

### Can I keep copies of employee records on my laptop?

Only where required for your role, protected in accordance with company security requirements, and removed when no longer needed.

---

### What should I do if I accidentally send personal information to the wrong person?

Report the incident immediately to the Information Security Team and Legal & Compliance. Do not attempt to conceal the mistake.

---

### Can customers ask to see the information we hold about them?

Yes. Requests should be referred to the Legal & Compliance Department for review and processing.

---

### How quickly should a privacy incident be reported?

Employees should report suspected privacy incidents within **one hour** of becoming aware of them.

---

### Can I access another employee's HR records?

Only if your role specifically requires access and you have appropriate authorization.

---

# 21. Policy Exceptions

Any exception to this policy must:

- Be documented.
- Include a business justification.
- Identify associated privacy risks.
- Be approved by the Head of Legal & Compliance.

Approved exceptions are reviewed periodically and withdrawn when no longer required.

---

# 22. Questions

Questions regarding this policy should be directed to the Legal & Compliance Department.

Privacy incidents should be reported immediately to the Legal & Compliance Department and the Information Security Team.
